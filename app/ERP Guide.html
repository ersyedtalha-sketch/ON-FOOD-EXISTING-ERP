The big picture

One simple flow, start to finish

Everything in the tool follows the way the business actually runs. Raw material comes in, becomes a product, gets sold, and the money is tracked. Five steps:

1

Buy raw material → Inventory

We record the grain (or makhana, mango, chilli) we purchase — quantity, cost, and transport. This is our stock on hand.

↓
2

Make the product → Batches

We take some raw material, send it for grinding, prepare the product, and record it as a batch. The raw material is automatically reduced from stock.

↓
3

Ready to sell → Products

Whatever we produce appears here as finished goods, with how many we have and what each one cost us to make.

↓
4

Sell it → Sales & invoices

We make a GST bill for the customer. Stock is reduced by what we sell. The bill can be saved as a PDF for our records.

↓
5

Collect money → Payments & credit ledger

When the customer pays — now or after a week — we record it. The ledger always shows who still owes us, and how much.

Why it matters

Because each step feeds the next, the numbers are always honest. You can never sell more than you made, the money owed is always correct, and at month-end everything needed for GST filing is already in one place.

02   The home screen

Overview

The first screen after login. A quick health check of the business — no data entry here, just the live picture.

What it shows

  • Billed — total value of all invoices raised.
  • Collected — money actually received.
  • Outstanding — money still to come in.
  • Overdue — bills past their due date, not yet paid.
  • Production snapshot — raw materials, inventory value, batches made, and units produced.
  • Products by units produced — what we've made the most of.

Download report — the button at the top right exports the whole business to an Excel file (one tab each for Inventory, Batches, Products, Partners, plus a Summary). Useful for the accountant or a monthly review.

Overview

03   Step one of the flow

Inventory — the raw material we buy

Every time we purchase grain, makhana, mango, or chilli, we record it here. This is our stock on hand — the starting point for everything we make.

To add raw material

  1. Click Add raw material.
  2. Enter the name (e.g. Juwar Grain), the vendor, and the month of purchase.
  3. Enter the quantity (in kg), the cost per kg, and the transport cost per kg.
  4. Save. It now shows in the list with stock left.
  • Stock left automatically goes down as you use the material to make batches.
  • The tool will stop you from making a batch bigger than the stock you have.
Inventory

04   Step two of the flow

Batches — turning material into product

A batch is one round of production: take some raw material, process it, and record what came out. This is where cost is calculated and product is created.

To make a batch

  1. Click New batch.
  2. Pick the raw material from inventory (only what you have in stock appears).
  3. Choose the product to make, or type a new product name (e.g. Juwar Roti).
  4. Enter how much raw material you're using (e.g. 50 kg) — it warns you if you exceed stock.
  5. Enter how many products per kg (e.g. 25 roti per kg → 50 kg makes 1,250).
  6. Enter the grinding/flour cost per kg and any miscellaneous cost (labour, packaging).
  7. Give it a batch number, set the status, and Save.

The tool then works out the total output, the cost per piece, and the total batch cost — and reduces the raw material from inventory. The new product appears in Products, ready to sell.

  • Made a mistake? Each batch has an edit (pencil) and delete button. Editing recalculates stock correctly; deleting returns the material to inventory.
Batches

05   Step three of the flow

Products — what's ready to sell

This screen is built automatically from your batches. It's the finished-goods shelf: what we have, and what it cost us.

Each product shows

  • Produced — total made across all batches.
  • Sold — total sold through invoices.
  • In stock — what's left (Produced minus Sold).
  • Cost per unit — what each piece cost us to make.
  • Status — Ready or Out of stock.

There is no selling price here on purpose. Prices differ — a wholesaler pays less, a small shop pays a little more. So the price is set freshly on each invoice, per customer.

Products

06   Step four of the flow

Sales & invoices

This is where we bill a customer. The list shows every invoice and its collection status; New sale creates a proper GST tax invoice.

To raise an invoice

  1. Click New sale.
  2. Pick the customer (from Partners). Their GST number, if saved, prints on the bill.
  3. Add a product line: choose the product, set pieces per pack, quantity, and the price per piece.
  4. Check the totals and GST (split as CGST+SGST within Karnataka, or IGST outside).
  5. Set the due date — today, or a week / 10 / 15 days later if it's on credit.
  6. Click Issue invoice.
  • You cannot sell more than you have in stock — the line turns red and the button locks.
  • You cannot issue a ₹0 invoice — a price must be set on every line.
  • Each issued bill gets a unique number (INV-2026-XXX) and opens a PDF to save — named by the bill number, ready for GST filing.
Sales & invoices

07   Step five of the flow

Recording payments

A customer may pay straight away, or after some days. Whenever the money comes in — fully or in parts — we record it against their invoice.

To record a payment

  1. Go to Sales, find the invoice with a balance.
  2. Click Record payment.
  3. Enter the amount received (use "Full balance" or "Half" for quickness).
  4. Choose the method — Cash, UPI, Bank, Cheque — and the date.
  5. Save.

The invoice updates instantly: Paid if fully settled, Partial if part-paid. If a due date passes with money still owed, it shows Overdue on its own — no need to track it by hand.

Payments

08   The money view

Credit ledger — who owes us

The complete record of money. Every invoice and its payment are clubbed into one clear line so you can see, at a glance, what's settled and what's still due.

Each line shows

  • Sale — the invoice amount.
  • Paid — how much has been collected.
  • Balance — what's still owed.
  • Status — Paid, Partial, or Open.

The top shows totals — Sales on credit, Collected, and Outstanding. The right panel lists partners who still owe money, oldest first, so you know who to follow up with.

Credit ledger

09   The address book

Partners

Everyone we deal with, in four groups. Adding them once means they're ready to pick whenever you make a batch or a bill.

Supplier

Who we buy raw material from (grain, makhana, mango, chilli).

Grinding mill

Outsourced grinding. We can keep several and choose between them.

Customer

Small shops & kirana stores we sell to. Can carry a GST number.

Wholesaler

Bulk buyers. Also billable with GST.

  • Add a partner with New partner; pick the role, name, phone, location.
  • For Customers & Wholesalers you can add a GSTIN — it prints on their invoices. Leave it blank and no GST line appears.

Putting it together

A normal week, in order

If you ever forget where to start, follow this.

1

Material arrives

Add it under Inventory with cost and transport.

2

We produce

Record a Batch — stock drops, product is created, cost is worked out.

3

An order comes

Raise a New sale — set the price for that customer, issue the bill, save the PDF.

4

Money is received

Record payment against the invoice — now or whenever it comes.

5

End of month

Open Credit ledger to chase dues, and Download report for the accountant.

One rule to remember

Record the work as it happens — material in, batch made, bill out, money in. Do that, and the books take care of themselves.
